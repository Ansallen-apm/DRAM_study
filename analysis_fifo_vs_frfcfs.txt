Analysis of FIFO vs FR-FCFS with Small Bursts (256B) and No Refresh

Configuration:
- DRAM: LPDDR4-6400 x64
- Traffic: Single STL Trace Player (Strict Interleaving)
- Burst Size: 256 bytes
- Refresh Policy: NoRefresh (Ideal baseline)
- Trace Pattern: 0, 128KB, 256KB, 384KB, 256B, 128KB+256B...

Results (Utilization vs Buffer Size) - No Refresh:

| Buffer Size | FIFO Utilization | FR-FCFS Utilization |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 64 | 10.97% | 75.25% |
| 128 | 16.39% | 89.08% |
| 256 | 49.71% | 99.16% |

Comparison with Per-Bank Refresh:

| Buffer Size | FIFO (NoRef) | FIFO (PerBank) | FR-FCFS (NoRef) | FR-FCFS (PerBank) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| 64 | 10.97% | 10.13% | 75.25% | 69.18% |
| 128 | 16.39% | 15.12% | 89.08% | 82.07% |
| 256 | 49.71% | 45.25% | 99.16% | 90.84% |

Detailed Analysis:

1. Impact of Refresh:
   - Disabling refresh improved performance across the board, as expected.
   - For **FR-FCFS (Buffer 256)**, utilization jumped from 90.8% to **99.16%**. This indicates that with ideal scheduling and no maintenance overhead, the system is nearly perfectly saturated (bandwidth limited only by minimal turnaround times). The ~8-9% difference represents the cost of Per-Bank Refresh and its associated bank unavailability.
   - For **FIFO**, the improvement was proportional but small in absolute terms (e.g., 45% -> 49% at Buffer 256). The thrashing overhead dominates so heavily that removing refresh only reclaims a small fraction of bus time.

2. FIFO Scaling (No Refresh):
   - Even without refresh, FIFO performance remains poor (11% - 16%) for small buffers.
   - The jump to ~50% at Buffer 256 confirms the "pipelining/batching" effect seen earlier. When the buffer is large enough to absorb the input stream without backpressure, the controller can process the request stream more efficiently (likely due to simulation artifact batching), but it still hits a hard ceiling (50%) far below FR-FCFS (99%) because it cannot reorder to fix the row conflicts.

3. FR-FCFS Scaling (No Refresh):
   - FR-FCFS scales beautifully.
   - Buffer 64: 75% (Limited visibility).
   - Buffer 128: 89% (High visibility).
   - Buffer 256: 99% (Perfect visibility).
   - This proves that the FR-FCFS algorithm is highly effective at extracting near-theoretical maximum bandwidth from the DRAM, provided the request buffer is large enough to find the row-hit optimization opportunities.

Conclusion:
Disabling refresh reveals the true "Scheduling Efficiency" limit.
- **FR-FCFS** is capable of **99% efficiency** in this scenario (interleaved, small bursts) if the buffer is large enough (256).
- **FIFO** is structurally limited to **~50% efficiency** even under ideal buffering/no-refresh conditions due to its inability to mitigate row conflicts.
- The "cost of refresh" in this high-load scenario appears to be approximately **8-10%** of total bandwidth.
