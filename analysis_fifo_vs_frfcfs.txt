Analysis of FIFO vs FR-FCFS for 4-Master Sequential Read on LPDDR4-6400 (High Load)

Configuration:
- DRAM: LPDDR4-6400 x64 (tCK = 0.3125ns)
- Masters: 4
- Traffic: Sequential Read, 1KB Burst
- Offsets: 0, 128KB, 256KB, 384KB
- Master Clock: 1000 MHz
- Request Count: 100,000 requests per master (Total 400,000)
- Note: User requested 1M requests, but simulations were capped at 100k due to memory constraints. The results at 100k are highly stable and representative of steady-state behavior.

Empirical Results (DRAMSys Simulation - 100k Requests):
- FIFO Utilization: 32.23% (Bandwidth: 16.48 GB/s)
- FR-FCFS Utilization: 50.87% (Bandwidth: 26.00 GB/s)

Comparison with Short Run (1000 Requests):
- 1k Requests: FIFO 32.22%, FR-FCFS 50.83%
- 100k Requests: FIFO 32.23%, FR-FCFS 50.87%
- The results are virtually identical (difference < 0.1%).
- This confirms that the simulation reaches steady-state very quickly and the performance characteristics (thrashing vs reordering) are consistent over long durations.

Analysis:
- The system is in a stable state of saturation.
- FIFO: The controller is continuously servicing row misses. The pattern of 4 masters interleaving access to the same bank but different rows is perfectly periodic. The utilization is capped by the ratio of (Burst Transfer Time) / (Row Cycle Time + Burst Transfer Time).
- FR-FCFS: The scheduler is continuously finding batches of ~8 requests per master (limited by buffer size and arrival rate). The utilization is capped by the amortized overhead of opening a row for 8 bursts.

Conclusion:
Running for a longer duration (100x longer) confirmed the initial findings. The FIFO scheduler yields ~32% efficiency due to severe row conflicts, while FR-FCFS improves this to ~51% by effective reordering. Increasing the simulation length simply produced more of the same behavior, verifying the robustness of the initial short-run analysis.
